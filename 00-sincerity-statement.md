# Sincerity Statement, Humanity's Charter

> ### I don't have all the answers. That's the point.
>
> We are at a very important time for humanity. The productive tools of automation, clean energy, and computing mean that material scarcity could be permanently solved. I say “could” with urgency because most people alive today live in a high-scarcity environment. Rent is due on the first of the month. They might have medical debt, and they are exhausted from working one 12-hour shift 5 or 6 days a week, or 2 to 3 part-time jobs. Abundance is not here yet. It is something we have to organize and fight for.
>
> These potential gains will not distribute themselves. Left on corporate autopilot, automated wealth will pool in the hands of a few oligarchs while millions face economic erosion. My goal is a world where machines perform the work of survival so we can actually live.
>
> I have tried to lay out a map across 26 interconnected chapters (from energy EROEI and sortition democracy to restorative justice and wealth taxation). I used modern tools and models to help scaffold the initial baseline drafts to structure these ideas. But software cannot feel the weight of labor, and machine output cannot build human trust. A framework only becomes a living charter when human beings debate it, tear down its blind spots, and rewrite its terms from their own lived experience.
>
> What follows is a starting position to focus our entire effort. It is one proposal among many, written down so you have something real to push against. I do not offer it as scripture or "This is the way." I offer it as a direction.
>
> I will be wrong in several of these positions. That is deliberate and welcome. I am not asking for your applause or passive agreement. I am asking for your active dispute: submit amendments, organize working groups, bring your counter-proposals to the floor, and help build a charter worthy of human dignity.
