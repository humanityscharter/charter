# Chapter 23: Call to Action

*Building an abundance society together*

---

## Introduction: It Is Time to Act

Many major expansions of human liberty reached a point when the old arrangement became intolerable and another way became technically possible.

Machine intelligence, distributed energy, and automated fabrication create new possibilities for ending material deprivation. Those tools are not enough by themselves. Institutions still decide who has access, who bears the costs, and who holds power.

If we remain passive, automated productivity could harden into techno-feudalism: a closed society where private monopolies own computing grids, use algorithms to discipline workers, and extract tribute from a public without economic security or decision-making power.

Freedom will not arrive as a gift from technology executives who mean well or from national bureaucracies. It has to be organized, debated, and built from the ground up through municipal councils, mutual-aid networks, open-source repositories, and local energy cooperatives.

The proposal is here, and the tools are within reach. What we need now is organized action and courage.

This Charter lays out a comprehensive plan for an abundance society powered by AI and robotics. It explains:

- **What it looks like** (Chapter 1) - A day in the life of abundance
- **How it works** (Chapters 2, 4-13) - The foundational systems and core infrastructure
- **Why it's possible** (Chapter 3) - The philosophical and biological foundations
- **What stands in the way** (Chapters 14-17, 20) - The critical challenges and how to overcome them
- **How we get there** (Chapters 24-26) - The transition roadmap, local governance, and coalition strategies
- **How we measure success** (Chapter 21) - Metrics beyond GDP
- **What you can do** (Chapter 22) - Concrete actions to contribute

Now comes the most important question: **Will you act?**

## The Urgency

### Why Now?

**The Window is Opening:**
Together, these factors create new opportunities for coordinated action across nations and cultures.

| Factor | Current observation | Proposed opportunity, not a forecast |
|--------|---------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **AI Capability** | Capabilities have advanced, but reliability, energy use, and access vary by task | Could lower some production costs if systems are dependable and publicly accountable; near-zero costs across essential goods are not established |
| **Robotics** | Robots perform some physical tasks in controlled settings; broad deployment remains uneven | Could take on more dangerous or repetitive work as reliability and cost improve |

| **Global Connectivity** | Digital networks connect many people, while access remains unequal | Could support coordination at scale if access and governance improve |
| **Public Awareness** | Public debates about these technologies and their ownership are active | Could become organized support for democratic alternatives |

**The Risks of Inaction:**

| Risk | Consequence |
|------|-------------|
| **AI Concentration** | Power consolidates in few hands |
| **Climate Crisis** | Environmental collapse undermines everything |
| **Inequality** | Social instability, conflict, suffering |
| **Techno-Feudalism** | Most people permanently marginalized |
| **Lost Potential** | Genuine freedom and dignity remain out of reach |

**The Stakes:**
- Hundreds of millions of people living in extreme monetary poverty: the World Bank estimated about 838 million people below $3.00 per day in 2022, using 2021 purchasing-power-parity dollars[^ch23-world-bank-poverty]
- Planetary ecosystems at the breaking point (climate change, biodiversity loss, resource depletion)
- Human potential largely unrealized (talent wasted due to lack of opportunity)
- Future generations depending on decisions made today

**Global Perspective:**
These challenges and opportunities span all nations and regions. Industrialized nations may have technological advantages. Developing nations may have demographic advantages and fewer legacy infrastructure constraints. This invitation is open to people bringing wisdom, innovation, and leadership from every corner of the globe.

**The Choice:**
We can allow AI and robotics to concentrate power and deepen inequality, or we can use them to create abundance for all. This choice spans nations and cultures, including industrialized economies and developing nations, urban centers and rural communities. The path we choose will affect every region of the world.

The decision we make in this decade will shape civilization for centuries.

## The Vision

### What We're Building

An abundance society is not a distant utopia. It's a practical, achievable vision grounded in:

**Technical Reality (see Chapter 4 for technical details):**
- AI and robotics could reduce some production costs and perform many routine tasks, but near-zero costs for essential goods are not established
- Renewable energy can expand low-carbon capacity, subject to materials, storage, grid, land, and ecological constraints
- Automation could reduce many forms of undesirable labor
- Technology can help coordinate complex systems more efficiently when publicly accountable

### Planning Assumption: technological direction, open timing

The Charter's **Planning Assumption** is that AI and robotics will continue to replace production labor, renewable and fusion energy will support increasingly abundant energy, and advanced material science will reduce some forms of resource scarcity. This is a confident long-term direction for planning, not a dated forecast or automatic public benefit: timing, exact capabilities, safety, deployment, ownership, distribution, and social consequences remain uncertain and subject to evidence and review. Technology alone does not guarantee public control, fair access, ecological sustainability, or the fulfillment of rights.

**Human Reality:**
- People can flourish when freed from survival pressure
- Community and connection are fundamental human needs
- Purpose comes from chosen contribution, not forced labor
- Humans are capable of cooperation and generosity

**Moral Reality:**
- No one should suffer when abundance is possible
- Technology should serve humanity, not the reverse
- Prosperity should be shared, not concentrated
- Future generations deserve a better world

### The World We Want

**We are working toward a world where:**

- No child goes to bed hungry
- Healthcare is a right, not a privilege
- Education liberates rather than sorts
- Work is chosen, not forced
- Technology serves human liberation
- Nature is restored, not destroyed

This world is possible. We must decide whether we will build it.

## The Path Forward

### The 30-Year Roadmap

This action roadmap follows Chapter 24's four transition periods. The dates are shared reference points. Action groups and communities may move at different speeds, and any scale-up still depends on Chapter 24 readiness gates.

**Foundation (Years 0-8)**
- Build coalitions and movements
- Launch pilot programs
- Develop open-source AI infrastructure
- Advocate for policy changes
- Create demonstration projects

**Expansion (Years 8-16)**
- Scale successful pilots
- Implement major policy changes
- Build critical infrastructure
- Establish governance models
- Coordinate internationally

**Global Floor (Years 16-30; transformation workstream where communities choose it)**
- Work toward an expanded UBR rights floor globally, with local additions subject to democratic choice and ecological capacity
- Safe, useful, and accountable AI and robotics deployed where readiness evidence supports it
- Economic transition tested and revised as capacity and funding evidence develops
- Governance reforms adopted where lawful authority, public consent, and safeguards support them
- Global cooperation strengthening
- **Target:** work toward basic needs being reliably met globally through both UBR tiers, subject to adopted rights floors, capacity, and evidence
- Work becomes more voluntary as both UBR tiers become adequate and durable; this is a goal to measure, not a promise
- **Target:** measurable sustainability progress within ecological limits, with targets revised when evidence requires
- Human well-being prioritized
- Continuous improvement ongoing

**Maturity (Years 30+):** Continue maintenance, ecological stewardship, public review, and lawful revision; this is a scenario, not an automatic endpoint.

These are proposed targets and decision gates, not predictions. We walk the path through evidence, public review, and revision.

### Timeline Realism

**Potential Delays:**
- Political opposition from entrenched interests could slow policy implementation
- Technical challenges in AI alignment may require additional research time
- International coordination may take longer than expected
- Infrastructure buildout faces practical constraints (supply chains, workforce training)

**Acceleration Factors:**
- Rapid AI advancement could compress timelines significantly
- Growing public awareness may create political momentum faster than anticipated
- Crisis response (climate, economic) could catalyze rapid policy changes
- Open-source collaboration may accelerate technical development

**Flexibility:** The roadmap adapts as conditions change. Some areas may move faster than expected; others need more time. The work is to keep momentum across all fronts.

## Common Concerns

### Addressing Questions and Objections

**"Isn't this unrealistic?"**

There are real technical and economic starting points for abundance society. Its full feasibility still needs to be tested; it is not a settled fact. AI and robotics could lower some costs; renewable energy is cost-competitive in many new-generation settings. We have to decide what to build and show that it works.

**"What about human nature?"**

Humans are capable of both competition and cooperation, greed and generosity, violence and compassion. Our institutions shape which tendencies emerge. Cooperation depends on how people use and govern a system. Systems that reward cooperation can help it grow.

**"Won't this lead to laziness?"**

Lower survival pressure can give people more room to pursue meaningful work. Whether this happens broadly remains an open question. Artists, scientists, entrepreneurs, and caregivers can contribute from passion and purpose.

**"How is this different from other utopian visions?"**

Unlike abstract utopias, this vision is:
- **Technical** - Grounded in existing and emerging technologies
- **Economic** - Based on actual cost structures and resource availability
- **Practical** - Includes implementation roadmaps and transition strategies
- **Measurable** - Uses concrete metrics beyond ideology

**"What about incentives?"**

Incentives matter. Currently, our incentives reward value capture and concentration. Abundance society redesigns incentives to reward contribution, cooperation, and sustainability. Guaranteeing basic needs can give people more room to pursue meaning beyond survival.

**"Won't this require authoritarian control?"**

No. Abundance society requires democratic governance. The goal is distributed power instead of concentrated control. Open-source technology, local autonomy, and participatory decision-making are core principles.

## The Hope

### Why This is Possible

**Human History Shows That Change Is Possible:**
- Legal slavery was widespread and defended by powerful states; abolition movements won major legal changes, while forced labor and trafficking persist
- Women's voting rights were denied in many places; sustained organizing won suffrage in many countries, though political equality remains unfinished
- Anti-colonial movements dismantled many formal empires; colonial harms and unequal power did not disappear with independence
- Medical miracles were fantasy. Now they are routine, including vaccines, antibiotics, and surgery
- Global communication was science fiction. Now it is ubiquitous

These changes took generations of organizing, conflict, political work, and changing minds. People won changes once dismissed as impossible through sustained effort. None was inevitable.

**Change Happens When:**
- People envision alternatives
- Coalitions form around shared goals
- Technology enables new possibilities
- Leadership emerges at all levels
- Persistence helps people work through objections

**The Conditions Are Right:**
- **Some tools are ready; others need work** - AI, robotics, and renewable energy already have useful applications, but a universal abundance system still needs technical, economic, and governance proof
- **Need is urgent** - Growing inequality, climate crisis, and persistent poverty create compelling moral imperative
- **Awareness is growing** - Increasing public discourse around UBI, climate action, and tech ethics reflects shifting consciousness
- **Leadership is emerging** - Visionaries and organizers are stepping forward in technology, policy, and community spaces
- **Build networks** - This Charter invites people committed to abundance values to form networks and build together around the world.

Abundance is a choice we make.

## Glossary of Key Terms

**Abundance Society** - A proposed society where technology may help provide material prosperity for all members, with a lawfully adopted basic-needs rights floor and work increasingly transformed from necessity to choice

**Universal Basic Resources (UBR)** - A rights floor of core necessities (nutritious food and safe water, safe adequate housing, comprehensive physical and mental healthcare, and education and lifelong learning) plus universal enabling services (basic energy, free transportation to essential services and community life, reliable connectivity, adequate computing devices, necessary assistive technologies, and their support and replacement), provided without employment conditions or point-of-use charges. Local additions remain possible within ecological capacity. UBI or other cash support is a separate transitional supplement, not a synonym for UBR.

**Marginal Production Cost** - The cost of producing one additional unit of a good or service; AI and robotics might lower this for some tasks, but physical goods still need materials, energy, upkeep, and labor

**Undesirable Labor** - Work that is dangerous, repetitive, degrading, or otherwise harmful to physical or psychological health; a primary target for automation

**Doughnut Economics** - An economic model that operates within ecological boundaries while ensuring social foundations for all (see Chapter 18)

**Post-Scarcity** - A proposed condition where essential goods and services are reliably available to everyone without economic deprivation; it does not mean all materials or choices become unlimited
