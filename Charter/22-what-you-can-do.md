# Chapter 22: What You Can Do

*Practical steps for contributing to an abundance society*

---

## Introduction: You Don't Have to Wait

Reading about an abundance society can inspire two opposite reactions:

1. **Hope** - "This is possible. We can build this."
2. **Overwhelm** - "This is huge. What can I possibly do?"

The second reaction is understandable. Building a new civilization sounds like something that requires governments, billionaires, or mass movements. But the truth is different:

Every person can choose a way to contribute, and every contribution can help.

This chapter gives you steps you can take today to help build an abundance society. You do not have to be a politician, CEO, or activist. You have to care about the future and be willing to act.

This is an individual action plan, not a Charter transition schedule. Personal “today,” 90-day, and longer-term steps remain distinct from Chapter 24's transition periods; communities and institutions decide collectively whether any related workstream is ready to advance.

---

## Why Personal Action Matters

### Why Individual Action Matters

**The Myth:** "I'm just one person. What difference can I make?"

**The Reality:**
- Large-scale change can grow from individual actions joined together
- Movements depend on people choosing to act and organize
- Your actions influence others through example and ripple effects
- Personal change can help people sustain social change

**The Pattern:**

*Individual action can support local work; connected local work can help change systems.*

**Examples of Individual Actions Creating Change:**
- Rosa Parks refusing to give up her seat → Helped spark the Civil Rights Movement[^ch22-rosa-parks]
- Greta Thunberg's school strike helped mobilize a wider youth climate movement; it did not create that movement alone
- Open-source developers sharing code can help others build and adapt technology
- Community garden organizers can improve local food access and community ties; a garden alone cannot secure a city's food supply

[^ch22-rosa-parks]: The U.S. National Park Service records that Rosa Parks's December 1, 1955 refusal and arrest launched the Montgomery Bus Boycott and invigorated the struggle for racial equality. This supports the example without treating Parks as acting alone or erasing the organizing that preceded and followed her refusal: https://www.nps.gov/people/rosa-parks.htm

You are part of a network. Your actions can connect with other people's work and contribute to movements.

---

### The Three Levels of Action

**Level 1: Personal Transformation**
- Change your own thinking and behavior
- Live the values you want to see
- Educate yourself and others
- Build resilience and well-being

**Level 2: Community Building**
- Connect with like-minded people
- Create local abundance initiatives
- Support neighbors and build mutual aid
- Demonstrate alternatives in practice

**Level 3: Systemic Advocacy**
- Engage in policy and governance
- Support organizations working for change
- Use your professional skills for the common good
- Build coalitions and movements

**You can act at all three levels at the same time.** Start where you are and build from there.

### 2. Shift Your Mindset

How you see the world can shape what you do and how you take part in change.

**Mindset Shifts:**

| From | To | How to Practice |
|------|-----|-----------------|
| Scarcity thinking ("there's not enough") | Abundance thinking ("we can create more") | Focus on possibilities and solutions rather than limitations |
| Competition ("I must beat others") | Cooperation ("we succeed together") | Seek win-win outcomes and collaborative opportunities |
| Individual success ("my achievement") | Shared prosperity ("our flourishing") | Celebrate others' success and work toward cooperative goals |
| Short-term gain ("quick wins") | Long-term flourishing ("sustainable future") | Make decisions considering future generations |
| "Me first" | "We together" | Consider community impact before personal benefit |
| Cynicism ("nothing will change") | Hopeful action ("I can make a difference") | Take small actions and notice their effects |
| Passive consumption ("just watching") | Active creation ("making things") | Create something daily, even small |
| Fear of change ("what if it goes wrong") | Embrace of transformation ("what if it goes right") | Visualize positive futures and work toward them |

**Practices for Mindset Shift:**

1. **Daily reflection** - Question scarcity assumptions
2. **Gratitude practice** - Recognize existing abundance
3. **Future visualization** - Imagine abundance society
4. **Community connection** - Learn from others' perspectives
5. **Action orientation** - Focus on what you can do

**Action Step:** Each morning, ask: "How can I act from abundance rather than scarcity today?"

### 7. Practice Mutual Aid

**Mutual aid is abundance in action. It involves people supporting each other directly.**

**What is Mutual Aid?**

Mutual aid is a practice of reciprocal exchange of resources and support among community members. Unlike charity, which involves one-way giving from those with resources to those without, mutual aid recognizes that:

- Everyone has something to contribute, regardless of their circumstances
- All participants are both givers and receivers
- Solidarity, not paternalism, is the foundation
- The goal is building cooperative power and community resilience
- Relationships are horizontal, not hierarchical

Mutual aid has been central to how communities survive crises, from labor movements to disaster response.

**Mutual Aid Practices:**

| Practice | Example |
|----------|---------|
| **Resource Sharing** | Lending tools, space, equipment |
| **Skill Sharing** | Teaching, mentoring, consulting |
| **Care Support** | Childcare, eldercare, meal preparation |
| **Financial Help** | Emergency loans, expense sharing |
| **Emotional Support** | Listening, companionship, counseling |
| **Crisis Response** | Emergency assistance, disaster relief |

**Building Mutual Aid Networks:**
1. Start with your immediate circle
2. Establish clear agreements and expectations
3. Build trust through consistent action
4. Expand gradually to wider networks
5. Document and share best practices

**Action Step:** Create or join a mutual aid network in your community.

## Practical Barriers to Taking Action

Taking action can feel daunting. Here are common obstacles and how to overcome them:

**"I don't have enough time"**
- Start with just 15-30 minutes per week
- Combine actions with existing activities (e.g., discuss topics during walks)
- Small, regular actions can help people stay involved over time.

**"I'm just starting out / I don't know enough"**
- You don't need to be an expert to contribute
- Start learning while doing. Action deepens understanding.
- Connect with others who can mentor you

**"What if I fail?"**
- Failure provides valuable learning
- Most successful initiatives went through multiple iterations
- Focus on progress, not perfection

**"I feel overwhelmed by the scale of problems"**
- Break big problems into manageable pieces
- Focus on what you can directly influence
- Celebrate small wins to maintain motivation

**"I don't know where to start"**
- Pick one area that resonates with you
- Talk to people already involved in that area
- Start with low-commitment actions to explore

**"People in my community aren't interested"**
- Start small with just one or two people
- Lead by example. Others may join later.
- Consider online options if local engagement is limited

**"I've tried before and it didn't work"**
- Reflect on what didn't work and why
- Try a different approach or partner with others
- Persistence often requires multiple attempts

## The Chen Family's Journey

*This is a fictional narrative illustrating how sustained action over time can create systemic change. The outcomes described are illustrative, not factual claims.*

This is a possible lead-up to the family's 2047 snapshot. The five story years below follow local family and community organizing; they are not Chapter 24's transition timeline years or a promised schedule.

### How the Chen Family Started

**Year 1: Personal Transformation**
- David studied community cooperation and social science
- Maria deepened community hub work and shared resource programs
- Leo began self-directed learning with maker lab projects (first water system design)
- Sam started play-based exploration and early curiosity-driven learning
- Family reduced consumption by 25%

**Year 2: Community Engagement**
- Joined local sustainability group through the community hub
- Maria started neighborhood tool library at the hub (20 families → 100+ in two years)
- Attended local council meetings (David advocated for participatory budgeting)
- Hosted monthly abundance conversations at the hub
- Connected with like-minded families across the neighborhood

**Year 3: Professional Application**
- David proposed a UBR (Universal Basic Resources) pilot framework to the local council
- Maria expanded community hub programs: maker labs, tool library, conflict resolution facilitation
- Leo's water system design won community innovation challenge at the hub
- Sam continued self-directed learning through play-based programs
- Family volunteered 10 hours/week total

**Year 4: Movement Building**
- UBR pilot approved locally (building on David's framework)
- Tool library expanded to 100 families, ~$50K/year reduction in neighborhood tool purchases
- Leo's water system design shared via open-source platform, adopted by two districts
- Community hub education model spread through neighborhood network
- Built coalition across political divides through abundance conversations

**Year 5: Systemic Impact**
- Local community adopted an abundance metrics dashboard (tracking all seven dimensions)
- Regional AI governance framework created (informed by David's community research)
- Leo's open-source water system design adopted by three districts
- Community hub model scaled to three additional neighborhoods
- Coalition influencing national policy on UBR implementation

**The lesson:** Small, consistent actions build momentum. The Chen family did not begin by changing the world. They began with themselves and their immediate community.
