# Chapter 5: Governance and Decision-Making

*How people decide together while rights, evidence, and public accountability remain protected*

## The destination model and the transition

This chapter is the policy home for the Charter's proposed mature governance system. It is a draft constitutional design, not a description of current institutions. During transition, existing elected institutions may lawfully authorize pilots, appropriate funds, appoint temporary staff, and place charter or constitutional changes before the voters or other bodies that current law requires. Those present-day mechanisms are transition tools. They do not become the destination model.

In the mature system, no governing office is won through an election. No one campaigns or raises money for a governing seat because there are no governing seats to contest. Authority is distributed among citizen assemblies selected by sortition, constrained professional administration, advisory experts, independent courts, and independent oversight. This is not rule by experts or by an unelected bureaucracy. Citizens decide the public rules and budgets; professionals carry them out; courts bind every institution to constitutional rights.

The model applies at local, regional, national, and global levels. It keeps the already-ratified global treaty design narrow and consent-based. It also preserves pluralism: proposals remain proposals until the applicable lawful decision process adopts them.

## Introduction: The governance challenge

Governance is social infrastructure. It decides how power is shared, how conflicts are resolved, and whether a community can respond to change without sacrificing vulnerable people. A workable design must face several recurring pressures:

- **Majority domination:** A temporary majority can harm a minority if rights are not independently protected.
- **Elite capture:** Wealth, status, organizational control, or information advantages can bend a formal process toward a narrow interest.
- **Short-termism:** Institutions may favor visible immediate gains over infrastructure, ecological care, or safety whose benefits arrive later.
- **Polarization:** Communities can become too divided to examine evidence or revise a preferred position.
- **Apathy and unequal participation:** People with less time, money, safety, access, or confidence can disappear from a nominally open process.

The Charter's answer is not that every resident votes on every technical question. It is a visible chain of authority: rights constrain the whole system; ordinary residents serve in assemblies; professional administrators execute what the assemblies approve; experts improve the record; independent courts and oversight bodies expose and correct violations. The process is deliberately demanding where decisions are structural and deliberately bounded where operations or immediate life safety require speed.

## Why simple democracy is not enough

Raw majoritarian voting has no inherent answer to the tyranny of the majority. A 51% vote cannot be allowed to erase bodily autonomy, equal protection, due process, privacy, expression, association, or ecological safeguards. That is why constitutional rights and judicial review are not optional decorations.

Direct voting on every issue also creates an information and attention problem. No resident can study every question about AI safety, water systems, housing design, public health, or fiscal tradeoffs. A campaign-style contest can reward marketing over evidence. Standing assemblies give a smaller group time to hear competing testimony, ask questions, examine uncertainty, and deliberate in public. The lottery keeps that group from becoming a self-perpetuating political class.

Coordination creates a second problem. Local choices can shift costs to neighbors, future generations, or other countries. Subsidiarity therefore means deciding locally when possible, not ignoring spillovers. Regional and treaty-level assemblies coordinate only where the effects cross boundaries and only within a defined mandate.

The design also takes voter fatigue seriously. A citizen is not asked to cast an uninformed vote on every matter. Service is a supported civic duty, while public petitions and testimony keep agenda access open to everyone.

## Core principles for good governance

1. **Equal standing:** Every eligible person has equal legal status and an equal chance of being selected, with support that makes service realistic.
2. **Evidence and learning:** Decisions disclose evidence, uncertainty, dissent, cost, and rights impact.
3. **Accountability:** Decision-makers, administrators, experts, and reviewers have named duties and public records.
4. **Rights and safety:** Constitutional rights, due process, and ecological boundaries bind ordinary majorities and institutions alike.
5. **Resilience:** Authority can respond to emergencies without allowing emergency powers to become permanent.
6. **Legitimacy:** People can see who decided, what information was considered, and how to challenge a decision.
7. **Proportionality:** The intensity of a decision rule matches its structural, rights, ecological, and emergency consequences.
8. **Subsidiarity:** Decisions stay at the lowest effective level, with higher levels handling only the coordination the lower level cannot provide.

These principles can conflict. Equality can require support that costs money; speed can conflict with deliberation; local autonomy can conflict with cross-boundary effects. The Charter makes those tensions explicit instead of pretending that one rule resolves them all.

## Six layers of authority

| Layer | Canonical role | Hard boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Constitutional rights and mixed independent judiciary** | Establish and enforce rights, due process, ecological protections, and institutional limits. | Courts invalidate unconstitutional acts but do not choose ordinary policy or initiate cases. |
| **2. Sortition citizen assemblies** | Standing general assemblies decide laws, budgets, major appointments, and oversight at their level. Temporary issue assemblies investigate and recommend. | A political disagreement is never grounds to remove a member. Assemblies cannot violate Layer 1. |
| **3. Constrained professional public administration** | Qualified administrators execute approved laws, budgets, standards, procurement, records, and operations. | Administrators cannot legislate, veto, redirect budgets, control lotteries, overrule courts or assemblies, or extend their own power. |
| **4. Advisory expert councils** | Provide evidence, options, warnings, dissenting reports, and recommended safeguards. | Experts have no final veto, suspensive hold, delay power, or exceptional binding authority. |
| **5. Limited direct public ratification and petitions** | Public petitions place complete proposals on the open agenda. Constitutional changes receive a protected public referendum. | Ordinary policy referendums and political recalls are not destination-model decision routes. |
| **6. Subsidiarity and local autonomy** | The lowest level capable of handling an issue decides it, subject to rights, ecological limits, and higher-level coordination. | Local autonomy cannot evade shared rights, treaty obligations, or lawful review. |

### Layer 1: Constitutional floor and mixed independent judiciary

The constitutional floor protects bodily integrity and personal autonomy; freedom of thought, expression, association, and conscience; equal protection; due process and fair treatment; privacy and data rights; access to basic rights and services; and ecological protections for present and future people. These rights bind assemblies, administrators, experts, agencies, treaty bodies, and courts themselves. Community and cultural autonomy operates broadly within this floor, which no majority or community-consent process may waive for another person. Shared cultural institutions and common resources use democratic community consent inside the floor; individual decisions about bodies, belief, relationships, healthcare, reproduction, private expression, personal data, and participation require informed individual consent. Residence alone assigns no cultural or religious identity, and multiple affiliation, minority institutions, and no affiliation retain equal public services.

The mixed judiciary combines professional legal judgment with citizen impact review. Professional judges issue the binding legal ruling in a case properly brought. A separately selected citizen panel evaluates real-world impacts, fairness, and proportionality. Public reasons and dissents make disagreement visible. A formal panel disagreement triggers the larger appellate judicial panel and a newly selected citizen panel required by the canonical rule. The court can invalidate a rights violation and order a lawful remedy; it cannot draft the policy it prefers.

### Layer 2: Standing and temporary assemblies

The standing assembly is the durable public decision body at each level. A temporary assembly has a narrower investigative mandate and a sunset. Its value is depth: residents can hear evidence, question assumptions, and identify tradeoffs without turning every issue into an election campaign. The standing assembly remains accountable for the final policy choice.

Modern deliberative processes, including citizens' assemblies, citizens' juries, and deliberative polls, show that randomly selected groups can receive evidence and produce considered recommendations.[^ch5-oecd-deliberative-processes] These examples establish a plausible mechanism, not a guarantee that every design will be representative or that recommendations will be enacted. The Charter therefore requires independent selection, compensation, accessibility, public records, and a defined authority and appeal route.

[^ch5-oecd-deliberative-processes]: OECD, *Innovative Citizen Participation and New Democratic Institutions: Catching the Deliberative Wave* (2020), reviews representative deliberative processes and describes random selection and demographic stratification: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/innovative-citizen-participation-and-new-democratic-institutions_339306da-en.html

### Layer 3: Constrained administration

Operations need continuity, procurement knowledge, records, and professional competence. The answer is a merit-based civil service and bounded senior administrators, not a second sortition legislature. Staff can explain feasibility and execute an approved mandate. They cannot change the rights floor, selection rules, budget purpose, or emergency duration on their own.

Chapter 17 places two specialized, constrained-administration bodies inside this existing Layer 3: the professional safety authority and the technical appeals panel. The professional safety authority is not an elected office, expert council, court, political body, rights office, allocation body, operator, evaluator, or seventh layer, and it does not create a new layer; the technical appeals panel is likewise not an elected office or any of those other bodies and does not create an additional layer. The professional safety authority applies standing citizen-assembly-enacted law, thresholds, evidence rules, budgets, public compute-allocation criteria, prohibited boundaries, and moratoria to a named case. It may grant, deny, condition, suspend, or require correction of a Chapter 17 deployment authorization. It has no priority setting power. It cannot evaluate its own work or system. It cannot legislate, make policy, rewrite thresholds, waive failed evidence or requirements, set compute priorities or social priorities, exercise a rights veto, control allocation, overrule an assembly or court, override Chapter 8, or extend its own powers.

The technical appeals panel conducts fresh administrative review under the same published rules, evidence standards, and evaluation criteria. It may only affirm, reverse, or remand a denial, condition, or suspension and must publish reasons. It has no priority setting power. It cannot replace a court or Chapter 8 review. It cannot make policy, waive criteria or evidence, set compute priorities or social priorities, operate or evaluate a system, review its own prior disposition, or serve as an expert veto. Passing technical safety review never substitutes for rights review, and passing rights review never substitutes for technical safety authorization. The independent rights office classifies rights effects, investigates, assists affected people, preserves evidence, publishes findings, and may seek emergency judicial relief; it has no policy veto. Courts make binding rights rulings and review legality, rights, evidence, proportionality, and sealing. Chapter 8 controls any rights-affecting AI decision.

Each body uses a separate instance of Chapter 5's established Layer 3 merit process: an independent civil-service office performs qualification and conflict review; a temporary citizen appointment jury selects 3–5 fully and equally qualified finalists without ranking them; a publicly auditable random draw selects one; and the standing assembly may ratify or reject only under the existing limited threshold and written-reason rules. The bodies have separate selection pools, reporting lines, budgets, records, and conflict controls. They remain structurally separate from operators, evaluators, affected applicants, the original decision-maker, and each other.

Terms are staggered and nonrenewable. No member may be immediately reselected, move directly between the safety authority and appeals panel, or take immediate employment with an operator the member regulated or reviewed. A transparent post-service cooling-off rule is published for each office and scales with the size of the qualified selection pool and the capture risk of the office, so small jurisdictions remain workable without weakening separation. The rule cannot be shortened for a named appointment.

### Layer 4: Advisory expert councils

Expert councils may cover AI and computing; ecological systems; resource, energy, water, and food systems; public health and medicine; infrastructure and transport; economic and financial systems; justice and safety; education and human development; technology and innovation; data and communications; governance and international relations; culture and wellbeing; regulatory safety; ethics and rights; and space or frontier risks. These are domains for evidence and advice, not offices with a binding vote. Councils should disclose methods, uncertainty, funding, conflicts, minority reports, and corrections.

### Layer 5: Limited public ratification

Public petitions keep agenda access open, but they do not bypass deliberation or rights review. Constitutional change is the sole destination-model direct referendum lane. A local pilot, budget, or ordinary law should not be sent to a referendum merely because a decision is difficult. The applicable standing assembly lane and appeal route remain the ordinary path.

### Layer 6: Subsidiarity

Local assemblies decide neighborhood and municipal matters when they can do so without imposing unreviewed harms beyond their boundary. Regional assemblies coordinate watersheds, transport, housing, energy, health, and other cross-local effects. National assemblies set standards and pooled capacities that lower levels cannot provide. Global bodies act only within ratified treaty scope. Higher-level standards should set rights and interoperability without absorbing local cultural or operational choices.

The office cannot veto lawful policy, create policy, or permanently restrict a person.

### Decision lanes

The neutral agenda office registers proposals from residents by petition, assembly members, issue assemblies, administrators, expert councils, and lower-level assemblies. It checks jurisdiction, format, signatures where required, cost and rights-impact materials, evidence completeness, accessibility, and conflicts. It cannot reject political content or substitute its judgment for the deciding assembly.

| Lane | Quorum, measured against filled seats | Approval | Route and safeguards |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Ordinary laws, budgets, appointments, and oversight** | 75% of currently filled seats | More than 50% of votes cast for or against; abstentions count toward quorum but not affirmative votes. | Standing assembly decides after the record, public testimony, and any relevant issue-assembly report. Approved remote and accessibility participation counts. Vacancies are excluded from the denominator and cannot count as participation. |
| **Major structural policy** | 85% of currently filled seats | 60% of votes cast for or against. | Standing assembly decides after a temporary issue assembly investigates and recommends, with rights and ecological review. |
| **Emergency-power renewal** | 90% of currently filled seats | Two-thirds of votes cast for or against. | Full standing assembly must publish a new justification. Renewal is not automatic. |
| **Constitutional change** | 90% of currently filled seats in each assembly | Two-thirds of votes cast for or against in the standing assembly and a separately selected constitutional assembly at the same thresholds, followed by independent rights review and a public referendum with at least 50% eligible-public participation and two-thirds of valid votes approving. | No ordinary policy referendum, recall, or assembly vote can substitute for this process. |
| **Local pilot** | The ordinary or major-policy lane that fits its rights, cost, and duration | Same lane threshold; temporary scope, sunset, evaluation, and appeal required. | During transition, an existing lawful elected institution may authorize a pilot. In the destination system, the relevant standing assembly authorizes it. |
| **Treaty or global decision** | As the ratified treaty specifies, preserving both chambers and any required domestic ratification | Double majority of the States Chamber and Peoples' Assembly; scope expansion requires formal amendment and renewed consent. | Independent treaty tribunal review, safeguards, proportionality, advance notice, appeal, and withdrawal remain mandatory. |

“Filled seats” means seats currently occupied through the lawful selection process. The quorum denominator is the number of currently filled seats only: vacancies are excluded from the denominator and cannot count as participation. An abstention by a seated member counts toward quorum but is not an affirmative vote. Remote participation counts when identity, accessibility, deliberation, and public-record requirements are met.

The ordinary appointment lane covers ordinary appointments other than the special lottery-selected senior-administrator and professional-judge processes. For those special processes, the public draw is presumptive. The standing assembly's limited ratification step may reject the drawn finalist only when at least 75% of currently filled seats participate in the rejection vote (vacancies are excluded from the denominator and seated abstentions count toward quorum) and at least two-thirds of votes cast for or against favor rejection. The written reasons must be limited to qualifications, ethics, conflict, or process integrity. A rejection must trigger the published replacement draw or other next lawful step; it cannot leave the office in indefinite procedural limbo.

## Citizen assemblies and sortition

Each governance level has a standing general citizen assembly. The standing assembly approves laws, budgets, major appointments, and oversight responses. A temporary issue-specific assembly is different: it investigates a major proposal through public and expert testimony, publishes alternatives and a recommendation, and then ends. The standing assembly makes the final decision.

Selection is a direct, independently administered, publicly auditable, stratified civic lottery from the full eligible population at that same level. Stratification may include geography, age, gender, income, disability, and other locally relevant factors. It shapes the pool and measures inclusion; it never permits an official, party, administrator, expert, or sponsor to choose a person.

Service is a presumptive civic duty like jury duty. Every member receives full compensation, employment protection, accessibility support, caregiving support, training, translation where needed, independent information, and safe participation conditions. Defined health, disability, caregiving, severe-hardship, safety, and conflict-of-interest deferrals or exemptions are available through a neutral process. A person cannot be penalized for a good-faith request.

Terms are 18 months and staggered. There is no consecutive service and no upward political career or delegation ladder. The cooldown is calculated at the same governance layer from the eligible-population-to-seat ratio:

- fewer than 20 eligible people per seat: 3 years;
- 20 to 100 eligible people per seat: 6 years;
- more than 100 eligible people per seat: 12 years.

Removal is possible only for corruption, concealed conflicts, serious misconduct, incapacity, persistent nonattendance, or unlawful disclosure. An independent ethics tribunal investigates. A separately selected citizen adjudication panel makes the final removal decision after notice, evidence, counsel or support, an opportunity to respond, and a written reason. Political disagreement is never cause.

## Constrained professional public administration

Layer 3 is not an administrative assembly. Senior public administrators are professional, nonpolitical executors. An independent civil-service office verifies published qualifications, ethics, and conflicts, using anonymized early screening where practical. A temporary citizen appointment jury conducts structured interviews using a published rubric and identifies 3–5 fully and equally qualified finalists without ranking them. A publicly auditable random draw chooses one finalist. The standing assembly ratifies or rejects the drawn finalist. Rejection requires at least 75% quorum measured against currently filled seats (vacancies are excluded from the denominator and seated abstentions count toward quorum) and at least two-thirds of votes cast for or against must favor rejection, with written reasons limited to qualifications, ethics, conflict, or process integrity. The published replacement draw or next lawful step must proceed after a lawful rejection.

The senior administrator serves a six-year, nonrenewable term, files annual public performance reports, undergoes an independent midterm review, and may be removed for defined cause or a properly supported no-confidence process with due process. Rank-and-file civil service remains merit-based under transparent rules; ordinary employees are not separately appointed by the assembly.

Administrators may manage staff and operations, execute approved laws, budgets, and standards, procure goods and services, coordinate agencies, maintain records, publish reports, and make bounded operational decisions delegated by law. They may not enact or veto laws, change rights or selection rules, create or materially redirect budgets, overrule assemblies or courts, extend their own authority or emergency powers, control civic lotteries, conceal decisions or conflicts, or convert implementation discretion into policy-making.

## Experts, courts, oversight, and enforcement

Expert councils are strictly advisory. They may provide evidence, options, warnings, dissenting reports, recommended safeguards, uncertainty, and conflicts. They have no final veto and no suspensive hold or delay power. Citizens decide, subject to constitutional judicial invalidation.

Courts have binding authority to invalidate assembly or administrative decisions that violate constitutional rights. An ordinary assembly vote cannot override a judgment. The lawful remedies are revision, appeal, or the protected constitutional-amendment process. Courts cannot initiate cases or create policy. Proceedings, written reasons, and dissents are public except for necessary privacy protections, and rights or emergency challenges receive immediate access.

Professional judges are selected through the qualified-finalist, temporary citizen-jury, public-lottery, and limited-ratification method adapted to legal qualifications. The public draw is presumptive; the standing assembly may reject only when at least 75% of currently filled seats participate in the rejection vote (vacancies are excluded from the denominator and seated abstentions count toward quorum) and at least two-thirds of votes cast for or against favor rejection. Written reasons are limited to qualifications, ethics, conflict, or process integrity, and the published replacement step follows a rejection. Terms are staggered, 12 years, and nonrenewable. Major constitutional and civil-rights cases include a separately randomly selected citizen review panel. Professional judges make the legal ruling. The citizen panel independently evaluates real-world impacts, fairness, and proportionality. Formal disagreement automatically triggers appeal to a larger appellate judicial panel plus a newly selected citizen review panel. Political recall is not a judicial accountability mechanism; defined-cause independent discipline and due process protect both the public and judicial independence.

Public agencies execute lawful decisions. Institutionally separate inspectors general, auditors, and ethics offices investigate misconduct and report directly to citizen assemblies. They may initiate investigations under law but do not make policy. Agencies cannot be the final reviewer of their own conduct. Courts authorize or review coercive enforcement where rights are affected and hear appeals.

## Emergencies

Professional emergency administrators and incident specialists may take immediate, narrowly defined life-safety actions under preapproved protocols. A rotating emergency committee drawn from the standing citizen assembly reviews within 24–48 hours and may approve, narrow, or terminate continued extraordinary measures. The full standing assembly reviews within seven days. Courts remain available for immediate rights review.

Extraordinary powers expire automatically after 30 days. Renewal requires 90% quorum of currently filled seats and two-thirds approval by votes cast for or against, with a new public justification. Emergency powers cannot permanently change laws, suspend core rights, delay civic-lottery rotation, suppress lawful dissent, or make themselves permanent. Public decision, evidence, expenditure, and conflict records are required, followed by an independent after-action audit.

## Global governance

The global model remains a consent-based treaty system, not a general world government. Scope is narrow and enumerated: climate and cross-border pollution; pandemic reporting and coordination; catastrophic AI safety standards and verification; cross-border financial secrecy and tax evasion; and governance of voluntarily pooled global funds, shared technology resources, or similar treaty-created commons. Adding subject matter requires formal treaty amendment and renewed domestic ratification.

The States Chamber has one vote per participating state. Each national citizen assembly determines its state's position. A rotating, randomly selected delegation presents and casts that instructed vote; delegates have no independent policy mandate. The Peoples' Assembly is directly selected through global stratified sortition from participating populations, with transparent caps, floors, and diversity safeguards. Binding treaty decisions require the ratified double-majority rules. Independent experts advise. The treaty tribunal reviews legality, proportionality, safeguards, appeals, and withdrawal. No elected or appointed diplomatic governing office is required in the destination system.

## Transition and local implementation

Chapter 25 is the local implementation playbook for this chapter. During transition, present-day elected councils, commissions, legislatures, or other lawful institutions may authorize pilots and place charter changes before the process current law requires. Campaign finance reform, public education, participatory budgeting, and advisory assemblies can be transition workstreams. They must be labeled as transition mechanisms, use legal review, protect existing services and rights, and never be described as the mature architecture.

Chapter 24 supplies the transition readiness gates. A pilot may go forward, be revised, paused, or stopped based on published evidence, affected-community participation, legal authority, costs, rights impacts, ecological limits, accessibility, and an appeal route. Demonstrating value never automatically transfers authority.

## Why this model is worth testing

Sortition can reduce a permanent political class, but a lottery alone does not guarantee fairness. The safeguards matter: a full-population public draw, stratification, support, compensation, transparent evidence, rotation, conflict rules, independent review, and judicial rights protection. Deliberation gives citizens time to understand difficult choices. Professional administration gives decisions operational continuity without giving administrators a policymaking mandate. Experts improve evidence without acquiring a veto. Courts and independent oversight make the boundaries real.

The design is intentionally slower for structural decisions and deliberately faster for bounded operations and life-safety emergencies. Its central promise is not that disagreement disappears. It is that disagreement is handled through a visible, lawful process in which citizens decide, professionals execute, experts advise, and rights remain enforceable.

## Deliberation and public decision process

For a major structural proposal, the normal sequence is:

1. A resident, assembly member, temporary issue assembly, administrator, expert council, or lower-level assembly submits a complete proposal.
2. The neutral agenda office verifies jurisdiction, format, required signatures, cost and rights-impact materials, accessibility, evidence completeness, and conflicts. It cannot reject a proposal because it dislikes its political content.
3. The record is published in accessible formats, with translations and a clear statement of what is established evidence, plausible mechanism, draft policy option, illustrative number, scenario, target, or open question.
4. A temporary issue-specific assembly hears affected communities, proponents, critics, public agencies, and competing experts. It may request records and publish alternatives, dissent, uncertainty, and a recommendation.
5. The standing assembly deliberates in public. Members receive independent facilitation, training, balanced briefings, time to question claims, and a protected route to request additional evidence.
6. The standing assembly classifies the decision lane, confirms quorum against currently filled seats, records abstentions, and votes under the applicable threshold.
7. Independent rights, ecological, fiscal, ethics, or treaty review occurs where required. A court may invalidate a decision for constitutional or rights violations, but it may not substitute its ordinary policy preference.
8. A professional administrator publishes an implementation plan, procurement and conflict disclosures, milestones, and a service-continuity plan. The administrator executes within the approved mandate.
9. Inspectors, auditors, and ethics offices monitor execution independently and report to the standing assembly. Affected people retain administrative and judicial appeal routes.

The exact timetable depends on complexity. The process is not a promise that every major decision takes a fixed number of months. It is a requirement that speed never erase the record, participation, rights review, or a route to challenge.

## Governance mechanisms and safeguards

### Sortition and inclusion

Sortition is not synonymous with a lottery conducted casually. The eligible population, exclusions, stratification factors, contact process, replacement rules, acceptance rates, and random draw must be published and independently auditable. Stratification may include geography, age, gender, income, disability, language, and other locally relevant factors. It shapes the pool but cannot authorize an official to hand-pick a person.

Members need full compensation, employment protection, accessibility support, caregiving support, safe transportation or remote participation, translation, training, independent information, and confidential help with conflicts or hardship. A person who cannot serve because of a defined health, disability, caregiving, severe-hardship, safety, or conflict reason needs a neutral deferral or exemption route, not a presumption of bad faith.

Rotation prevents a permanent civic class, but continuity still matters. Staggered 18-month terms, documented handovers, public records, and professional staff preserve institutional memory without giving staff or former members a policy mandate. The population-to-seat cooldown rule provides a transparent anti-entrenchment safeguard that adapts to local scale.

### Deliberative practice

Facilitators should separate fact claims from values, distinguish uncertainty from disagreement, prevent intimidation, and make room for minority positions. Public testimony is not a popularity contest. Expert evidence should include competing methods and dissent. A decision record should state which evidence was accepted, which remained uncertain, what tradeoffs were considered, and what would trigger revision or pause.

### Thresholds and abstentions

Thresholds are calibrated by lane. Ordinary work should not be impossible, but structural or emergency decisions require broader participation and support. Quorum is always calculated from currently filled seats. A vacant seat is outside the denominator and cannot count as participation. A seated member's abstention counts toward quorum but not toward affirmative approval. Remote participation counts when identity, accessibility, deliberation, and record requirements are met.

### Transparency and privacy

Public records include agendas, selection audits, testimony, evidence, votes, rationales, budgets, procurement, conflicts, implementation reports, and oversight findings. Personal addresses, health information, safety details, confidential legal advice, and other narrowly necessary privacy protections may be withheld or redacted. Transparency is not a license to expose people to retaliation.

### Independent oversight

An inspector general, auditor, ethics office, records office, and ombuds or rights advocate may have different mandates, but none should be captured by the agency it reviews. They can initiate investigations under law, preserve evidence, publish findings, and refer matters to assemblies or courts. They cannot make ordinary policy or act as an expert veto. Agencies execute lawful decisions; they do not certify their own misconduct as resolved.

### Removal and discipline

Assembly members are removable only on the defined causes in the canonical rule and only after independent investigation and a separately selected citizen adjudication panel's due-process decision. Professional administrators and judges have defined-cause discipline and due process. Political disagreement, an unpopular vote, or an adverse expert opinion is not misconduct.

### Minority and future-generation protection

Rights review, accessible participation, descriptive inclusion through stratification, public-interest evidence, ecological boundaries, and appeal protect people who may be outnumbered or absent from a meeting. A future-generations advocate or ecological office may provide evidence and raise a review request, but cannot acquire an unbounded policy veto. The standing assembly remains responsible for the decision within rights and ecological constraints.

## Preventing common failure modes

### Mob rule and majoritarian harm

The response is a rights floor, deliberation before structural decisions, public evidence, high participation thresholds, independent judicial review, and a remedy route. A standing assembly cannot vote away protected rights.

### Elite capture

The response is a full-population lottery, auditable stratification, no candidate campaigns for assembly seats, no consecutive service, population-based cooldown, public conflicts, independent oversight, transparent records, and compensation that makes participation feasible for people without wealth.

### Short-termism

The response is staggered service, long-horizon impact records, ecological limits, infrastructure review, readiness gates, and revision conditions. The model does not assume that a longer term alone creates wisdom.

### Inefficiency and gridlock

The response is a clear lane map, a neutral agenda office, professional execution, subsidiarity, bounded delegation, and emergency protocols. Administrators can act within an approved mandate without becoming lawmakers. Courts can resolve rights questions without deciding ordinary policy preferences.

### Polarization

The response is structured contact among residents who would not otherwise meet, balanced testimony, facilitation, protected dissent, and written reasons. Agreement is not required for a legitimate process, but the process must show that disagreement was heard.

### Apathy and unequal access

The response is civic-duty framing, full compensation, protected leave, caregiving, disability access, language access, safe remote participation, public education, and petition routes for people not currently serving.

## Equality and accountability

Equal voice is not only equal voting weight. It includes equal opportunity to be selected, equal ability to understand the record, equal protection when disagreeing, and equal access to a remedy. Stratification is a safeguard against predictable exclusion, not permission to allocate seats by official preference.

Accountability has several directions:

- The assembly answers to the public record and constitutional rights.
- Administrators answer to the assembly's lawful mandate, civil-service rules, independent midterm review, audits, and courts.
- Experts answer for methods, conflicts, uncertainty, and corrections, not for whether citizens adopted their preferred option.
- Inspectors, auditors, and ethics offices answer for independence, due process, and public reporting.
- Courts answer through written reasons, open proceedings where safe, appellate review, and judicial discipline that respects independence.

## Global coordination without world government

Cross-border governance remains consent-based. A state enters a defined treaty through valid domestic ratification and is bound only within the treaty's enumerated scope. That scope does not automatically cover ordinary domestic policy, culture, or every global concern.

The States Chamber gives each participating state one instructed vote. The national citizen assembly determines the state position; a rotating randomly selected delegation presents and casts it without an independent mandate. The Peoples' Assembly is directly selected through global stratified sortition from participating populations, with caps, floors, translation, accessibility, and anti-domination safeguards. Binding treaty decisions require the ratified double-majority rules. Experts advise; they do not cast a binding vote.

An independent treaty tribunal reviews jurisdiction, evidence, proportionality, resident protections, essential services, and due process. A graduated enforcement sequence begins with notice and a transparent finding, assistance and a corrective plan, monitored compliance, and only then a proportionate treaty-authorized consequence. Interim measures for imminent cross-border harm are necessary, time-limited, and promptly reviewed. Members retain advance-notice withdrawal, subject to obligations already adjudicated or expressly time-limited.

This model coordinates climate and cross-border pollution, pandemic reporting and coordination, catastrophic AI safety verification, cross-border financial secrecy and tax evasion, and voluntarily pooled global funds or shared technology resources. New subject matter requires formal amendment and renewed domestic consent.

## Implementation and readiness roadmap

Chapter 24's four transition periods control timing. Governance work is nested within those periods and is not a rival schedule:

- **Foundation, Years 0–8:** Map legal authority, establish rights and oversight baselines, run lawful local and regional pilots, test lottery administration, compensate participants, and publish evidence.
- **Expansion, Years 8–16:** Where readiness gates pass, extend standing assemblies and professional administration to additional jurisdictions, test cross-local coordination, and build accessible records and appeal capacity.
- **Global Floor, Years 16–30:** Extend the model to participating national and global treaty bodies where consent, capacity, rights, and anti-domination safeguards are present. No jurisdiction is required to advance automatically.
- **Maturity, Years 30+:** Review performance, participation, rights outcomes, administrative quality, ecological effects, and remaining gaps. Amend only through the lawful constitutional process.

Each workstream must identify a responsible authority, baseline, evidence, affected communities, cost, rights and ecological impacts, uncertainty, review date, and GO, REVISE, PAUSE, or STOP/REPLACE condition. A successful advisory pilot does not itself authorize binding authority.

## Common concerns

### “Won't this be too slow?”

Structural decisions are intentionally slower because reversal can be costly. Ordinary operations remain with professional administrators under published rules. Emergencies use preapproved protocols, a 24–48-hour citizen review, a seven-day full-assembly review, court access, and an automatic 30-day expiry. Speed is bounded by rights and records rather than by a blank check.

### “Won't experts still dominate?”

Experts can influence any society through information, but this design makes their role visible and contestable. They publish methods, evidence, uncertainty, conflicts, alternatives, and dissent. They do not decide, veto, hold, or delay. Citizens decide after deliberation, and courts review only legality and rights.

### “Won't people refuse to participate?”

Service is a presumptive civic duty like jury duty, with real compensation, protected employment, accessibility, caregiving, safety, and neutral deferral rules. Acceptance rates, exemptions, and replacement patterns are public so the lottery's practical representativeness can be assessed rather than assumed.

### “What prevents gridlock?”

The lane map distinguishes ordinary work, structural policy, emergencies, constitutional change, local pilots, and treaty decisions. The neutral agenda office ensures complete records without censoring political content. Professional administration keeps implementation moving within the mandate. If a proposal cannot pass, the record can identify whether the problem is evidence, rights, resources, or public disagreement.

## Chen family example

At the neighborhood level, David joins a compensated local issue assembly on a community kitchen and shared garden. Residents explain access needs, gardeners provide evidence, and an administrator reports cost and maintenance constraints. The issue assembly recommends two layouts. The standing local assembly chooses one in the ordinary lane and publishes reasons, budget, and a review date.

Later, Maria is selected for the regional standing assembly. She does not campaign for a seat and cannot build a political career through successive appointments. She serves 18 months with protected leave and caregiving support, then waits through the applicable population-based cooldown. A temporary housing issue assembly hears tenants, builders, disability advocates, environmental experts, and critics. The standing assembly makes the final policy choice. A professional administrator procures the approved homes and reports publicly. If a resident claims the policy violates a constitutional right, the court can invalidate it or order a lawful remedy; it cannot choose a preferred housing policy.

## Architecture synthesis

Each of the six layers has a distinct job:

1. **Constitutional rights and mixed judiciary:** establish boundaries and provide binding rights review.
2. **Standing sortition assemblies:** decide public rules, budgets, major appointments, and oversight.
3. **Constrained professional administration:** execute approved decisions and operate services.
4. **Advisory expert councils:** provide evidence, options, warnings, dissent, and safeguards.
5. **Limited public ratification and petitions:** reserve referendums for constitutional ratification while keeping the agenda open.
6. **Subsidiarity and local autonomy:** keep decisions close to affected people while coordinating genuine spillovers.

The model connects to AI infrastructure through public oversight and safety review; to the economy through budgets, UBR, and transparent resource choices; to education through civic capacity; to privacy through data minimization and public records boundaries; to ecology through binding limits; and to global equity through treaty safeguards.
